# Research — Mobile Fragrance Discovery

## Problem frame
Online perfume purchase has an information asymmetry: the user cannot smell the product. The design therefore cannot rely on bottle image + price as the primary decision model. Violet should reduce uncertainty through language, context, sampling, explainability and post-trial feedback.

## Current reference evidence

### Byredo
Observed official discovery-set behavior:
- curated 6 × 2 ml discovery sets;
- category-led sets such as floral / woody;
- customizable discovery selection exists;
- discovery-set purchase can include a time-limited voucher toward a full-size fragrance.

Transfer principle: discovery is a product in its own right and can bridge sample → bottle. Violet may prototype this bridge, but any redemption/credit must be explicitly labelled fictional.

### Le Labo
Observed official discovery behavior:
- 1.5 ml and 5 ml sample sets;
- full classic-collection exploration as a deliberate journey;
- some sets package discovery with a future 50 ml choice;
- personalization of packaging is part of the ritual.

Transfer principle: make exploration feel intentional and finite, not like a giant catalogue.

### Frédéric Malle
Observed official discovery behavior:
- customizable Discovery Trio;
- sample trio can be redeemed toward a later perfume purchase;
- a perfume finder is exposed as a dedicated discovery path.

Transfer principle: trio-building and guided discovery should be primary IA, not utility links hidden under shop navigation.

### Luckyscent
Observed official sample/discovery behavior:
- sample catalogue can be filtered by brand, gender, main note, style, fragrance notes, concentration, price, sample size and country;
- custom Fragrance Fitting asks about occasion, loved/loathed scents and preferred notes;
- themed sample packs reduce the burden of browsing a large sample catalogue.

Transfer principle: vocabulary-rich filters and contextual questions complement each other. Experts can filter directly; unsure users can use guided fitting.

## Product hypothesis
A mobile-first flow that begins with context and preference, explains every recommendation, lets the user build a balanced three-sample trial, and captures post-wear feedback will demonstrate stronger fragrance-product thinking than a generic ecommerce grid.

## Required journey
Discover → Scent Portrait → explainable recommendations → PDP → Discovery Trio → checkout prototype → try → feedback → refined profile → updated recommendations → full bottle.

## Marketplace role split
- Buyer: atmospheric, visual, low cognitive density, progressive disclosure.
- Seller: catalogue/order/batch operations with compact tables and clear states.
- Admin: curation/provenance/marketplace operations with the highest information density.

## Truth boundaries
- No AI/ML claim: scoring is deterministic and rule-based.
- No personalization-accuracy claim.
- No real customer or behavior claim.
- Fictional houses remain labelled as fictional prototype entities.
- No fulfilment claim.
- Any sample credit/redemption concept must be labelled prototype-only.
