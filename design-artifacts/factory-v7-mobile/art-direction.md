# Art Direction — Editorial Perfumery on Mobile

## Visual thesis
Violet should feel like a pocket perfumery journal, not a compressed desktop marketplace. The mobile surface is paced around object, skin, liquid, ingredient, shadow and scale. Product information appears in quiet layers around the image rather than competing with it.

## Signature
- warm paper ground, black ink, muted violet as an accent rather than a marketplace theme;
- serif editorial display paired with restrained sans for utility;
- asymmetric image crops and material studies;
- numbered editorial cues (`V.01`, `01 / WHY IT FITS`) to create continuity;
- thin rules, no soft-card-everywhere UI;
- tactile bottom sheets and pill-less choice rows;
- visual ratio target on discovery surfaces ≈70% image / 30% copy.

## Mobile composition
- opening viewport: one object or material study + one clear next action;
- Scent Portrait: calm single-question canvas, large choices, progress always visible;
- recommendation cards: image dominates, explanation is directly below the object, not hidden in a modal;
- PDP: image gallery → match explanation → bottle/sample decision → notes/context;
- trio: selected vials shown as three physical slots; balance is a simple textual/visual spectrum, not a fake analytics dashboard;
- feedback: feels like a private scent journal, not a survey form.

## Motion
- short opacity/translate reveal only after first paint;
- direct feedback on choice, replacement and save;
- respect `prefers-reduced-motion`;
- no motion required to understand state.

## Operational surfaces
Seller and Admin intentionally break from the atmospheric buyer surface. They use smaller type, compact controls, tables, status chips and higher information density. Luxury brand cues remain only in typography/ink discipline, not in large editorial imagery.
