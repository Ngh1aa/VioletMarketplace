# Brand and Visual Direction — Violet V4

## Creative thesis
**Violet is a private fragrance library with a sampling ritual, not a purple marketplace.**

V4 shifts from `warm-violet editorial ecommerce` to `quiet object study + curated fragrance library + tactile discovery ritual`.

## Visual attributes
1. **Object-led** — bottle/sample/packaging imagery gets more space and fewer competing labels.
2. **Quietly editorial** — asymmetric compositions and annotation rhythm appear where they clarify story, not everywhere.
3. **Warm but not pastel-heavy** — warm ivory is the dominant field; aubergine/ink provides authority; lilac and violet become controlled signatures.
4. **Tactile** — glass, paper, stone, skin, silk, wood and shadow define the media world.
5. **Precise** — rules, indexes, format labels and provenance read like a curated library, not badges.
6. **Selective** — fewer modules, fewer product cards per story, more intentional merchandising.
7. **Human-scale discovery** — sampling/wearing language over pseudo-scientific personalization.

## Color-role map
- Warm Ivory `#FFFDFB`: dominant canvas / approximately 55–65%.
- Violet Night `#251829`: header/footer/authority fields / approximately 15–20%.
- Aubergine `#4F3158`: text/action depth, not universal background.
- Pastel Lilac `#CFB2DF` / Lavender Mist `#F4EAFA`: orientation and selected discovery moments / approximately 10–15% combined.
- Warm Blush `#F1DFD5`: media warmth/support, not section filler.
- Violet Ink `#81548F`: rare interactive/signature accent.

No gold is required to signal luxury.

## Typography
- Keep a refined platform-safe serif for fragrance names/editorial statements and restrained system sans for interface.
- Reduce oversized serif repetition; only one dominant typographic moment per viewport region.
- Product cards should read faster: house → name → notes/format → price.
- Micro labels use uppercase sparingly; avoid turning every section into a numbered design case study.

## Composition signatures
1. **Campaign diptych** — one dominant object/image field + one concise decision panel.
2. **Curated shelf** — product rows/grids interrupted by a house/mood/discovery story, not endless equal cards.
3. **Fragrance dossier** — tall media object + compact commerce block first, deeper story later.
4. **House portrait** — editorial brand field + collection rail; each fictional house gets distinct accent/media treatment within Violet system.
5. **Discovery table** — samples, sequence and evaluation as a ritual/workspace.
6. **Consultation desk** — preserve Scent Portrait two-pane concept, but connect result to sampling.

## Recognition test
With logo removed, V4 should remain recognizable through at least four cues: warm-ivory/ink color behavior, object-study photography, serif/sans editorial rhythm, fine annotation/rule grammar, and sampling/house-centric composition.

## Do
- make photography/product object the focal evidence;
- use Violet color as a signature event;
- expose house/provenance and sample/full-bottle decisions early;
- vary silhouette by page role;
- allow negative space to frame product rather than decorate it.

## Do not
- glassmorphism/liquid-glass luxury clichés;
- repeated lilac gradient sections;
- universal `heading + three cards` modules;
- long manifesto copy to explain premium positioning;
- fake gold, fake scarcity or fake AI;
- copy a reference site’s surface treatment.
