## Benchmark synthesis

### Pattern 1 — Sampling is a product model
Strong fragrance references treat sampling/discovery as a purchasable, navigable format linked to a later bottle decision. V4 must not bury sample language inside PDP copy.

### Pattern 2 — Discovery uses multiple mental models
Family, mood, house, notes and expert guidance coexist. Violet should let novice and enthusiast users enter from different directions.

### Pattern 3 — Multi-house luxury needs retailer authority
Luckyscent/ZGO/Nose prove that curation, expert guidance, sample service and provenance are part of the retailer’s product. Violet needs Houses + Discovery service structure, not only a pretty grid.

### Pattern 4 — Product/commerce remains legible
Premium references can be editorial while still making size, price, sample/full-bottle and add action obvious.

### Pattern 5 — Brand storytelling should be sparse and located
Diptyque/Le Labo/Byredo establish a world through media/product/campaign systems. Violet should reduce repeated manifesto explanation and let media/composition carry more identity.

## Final reference jobs
- Commerce restraint: Le Labo / Byredo.
- Discovery architecture: Frédéric Malle / Aesop.
- Closed-loop sampling: Nose.
- Multi-house marketplace/service model: Luckyscent / ZGO.
- Sensorial storytelling / trial service: Diptyque.
